Shadows that know where the light is.

The light has a position in three dimensions. Objects sit at a height above the page, and their shadows are projected from that geometry rather than styled by hand.

Click anywhere to move the light.

Elevation

Four identical tiles at four heights. Nothing else differs. Offset and blur both come from height ÷ (lightHeight − height), so they stay in agreement without being tuned separately.

Light size

One number, --cast-light-size, moves the whole scene from hard midday sun to overcast. It is the penumbra width, so it changes softness without touching position.

Surface lighting

data-cast="surface" models the object's own face from both ends: highlight toward the light, shading away from it. Which end does the work depends on the surface, not the theme — white has almost no headroom on a pale panel, so this one is modelled almost entirely by its shaded corner. The angle comes from CSS atan2(), so none of it needs extra JavaScript.

Edge highlight

data-cast="edge" adds a hairline rim: lit on the side facing the light, shaded on the side away from it. One inset shadow each way, reusing the same vector. It costs almost nothing and does more for the illusion than anything else here.

Room lighting

data-cast="room" lights the space rather than the object: a pool centred on the lamp, falling off to a vignette at the edges. This whole page is in room mode — move the light and watch the background follow it. It works on any element too, so the panel below carries its own pool, clipped to its own box.

None of this needs JavaScript. The pool's position comes from numbers already on the element, and nothing here touches background-image — it composites over whatever backgrounds the element already had.
